How this works

Nothing on this page is stored. Every panel is a Virtual Cypher hop from a single pinned key, computed when you ask for it and cached afterwards.

The key

The whole chart hangs off one string, the way a chess analysis hangs off a FEN:

1962-05-17T14:30|51.5074,-0.1278|Europe/London
      date  time   lat,lon          zone OF THE BIRTHPLACE

Drop the T14:30 and the chart returns with no ascendant and no houses, because without a birth time there are none. The realm will not invent them. The zone is the one field people get wrong, and getting it wrong moves the rising sign by a whole sign — which is why Find resolves a place name to its coordinates and its historical IANA zone.

The views behind each panel

Each is a saved view in your world; this page sends the birth fields and renders the rows.

  • CastChart — the summary line and the honesty chips.
  • ChartPlacements — the ten bodies, and the planet positions drawn on the wheel.
  • ChartHouses — the twelve cusps, which are the spokes.
  • ChartAspects — the lines across the middle.
  • TransitsOnDay — today's sky against this chart. Today is computed in the page at load, never baked in.
  • AuditAgainstNasa — ten live requests to JPL. Button-triggered, because it is paced.
  • FindBirthplace — the place lookup.

The wheel itself is one of those view results drawn as SVG:

MATCH (b:BirthMoment {spec: $spec})-[:HAS_PLACEMENT]->(p:Placement)
RETURN p.body, p.position, p.sign, p.house, p.retrograde, p.dailyMotion
ORDER BY p.longitude

Where the numbers come from

Positions from astronomy-engine; houses, ascendant and the birthplace's historical time zone from deepnatal. Both are bundled into the realm's handler, so casting a chart needs no network at all. The two libraries compute positions independently and the chart reports how far they disagreed — that is the engines agree to chip, in arcminutes.

What the chips mean, and what an empty panel means

  • engines agree to N' — the worst disagreement between the two ephemerides, in arcminutes. Under a few arcminutes changes nothing astrologically; it is shown because a tool that hides its own error bars is asking to be trusted rather than checked.
  • no birth time — houses and ascendant are absent, so the Houses panel is empty and every planet's house is blank. That is the correct answer, not a failure.
  • whole-sign houses — Placidus is undefined inside the polar circles, so it was abandoned and the page says so. House positions there are a convention.
  • Moon's sign uncertain — with no birth time, the Moon moved far enough on the birth day to cross a sign, so its sign depends on the hour.
  • zone in doubt — another IANA zone is defensible for those coordinates. Resolve it before reading the houses.
  • An empty Transits panel means nothing is within orb today, which is rare but real. An empty NASA audit means you have not run it yet.

The astrologers

Four readers, each a prompt brief plus the realm's astrology-reading skill, calling gateway.astrology.askAstrologer. The facts are computed first and handed to the model — it never recomputes a position and is told not to introduce one that is not in the chart. A position is a fact and a reading is a judgement, so in the graph they are separate types with separate caches, and this page keeps them in separate panels for the same reason.

This panel is not your assistant's chat. The host's chat session is per-user and broadcast to every surface you have open, so it can wear one persona at a time; four readers disagreeing needs four threads that leave your session alone.

What the appliance did here that a conventional stack could not

There is no chart database and no nightly job. The first time anyone asks for a given birth moment, the world computes the chart, the placements, the aspects, the cusps and the transits, stitches them into a graph you can traverse, and caches them as immutable — because a chart is a deterministic function of an instant and a place, so the answer for a key is the answer forever. A second request is a graph read.

The same key reaches the same chart from stored birth data: a BirthRecord you keep joins to the identical computed NatalChart, so "my chart" is a question with no arguments. And SkyMoment is keyed on an instant and nothing else, which means anything in your world carrying a timestamp — a build, an invoice, a conversation — can be given the sky that stood over it. Those views compute their own base rate, because Mercury is retrograde about a fifth of the time and a figure without its denominator is a credulity machine.

Then the part no other astrology surface offers: AuditAgainstNasa asks JPL Horizons where each body actually was and reports the difference in arcseconds. The arithmetic is checkable rather than merely confident.

Astrology has no known mechanism and no reliable evidence behind it. This page computes a real configuration of the heavens and reads a symbolic tradition over it, and tries hard not to confuse the two. Ask Cassius.
